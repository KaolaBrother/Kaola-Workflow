## Context

Finalization was the one phase where the orchestrator had **less** freedom than mid-run.

The finalize prose prescribed a fix lane for a failed final validation (build/type/lint/tooling →
`build-error-resolver`; behavior/regression/coverage/test-defect → `tdd-guide`). The fix lands. Then
the finalize attribution sweep refuses it `unattributed_change`, because that sweep credits exactly
two sources: the narrow `.md` allowband, and the declared write sets of `complete` nodes. A fix
produced *during* finalization belongs to neither — the writer nodes are all discharged, and their
write sets were frozen before the failure existed.

On the planner's ordinary all-concrete spine, every other sanctioned exit is closed **by
construction**:

- `amend-surface` needs an expansion-point row, which an all-concrete spine does not have;
- `reopen-node` refuses `would_orphan_in_progress` over the live sink (`finalize` is not a gate role,
  so the sink is an orphan `in_progress` row, not a foldable post-dominating gate);
- a re-plan epoch needs a **failed** review attempt, and at finalization every review PASSED;
- `revert-overflow` discards, and cannot restore a new file.

So the prescription manufactured the very refusal that blocked it, and the refusal named no exit.
The measured cost was ~40 minutes of an orchestrator proving the exit set empty, ended by a user
interrupt.

## Decision

**Zero regulation on HOW the fix is produced; full regulation at the ONE commitment point where it
enters the candidate.**

1. **Free route.** During finalization, before the sink's first irreversible step, the orchestrator
   repairs a failed final validation however it judges best — inline for a trivial correction, or
   dispatched to whichever role fits. No mandated mode, no justifier, no approval attaches to the
   choice. The finalize prose was rewritten from a prescriptive routing table to that statement.

2. **One typed commitment: `final-fix-commit`.** A new `adaptive-node` subcommand records the fix into
   a digest-bound, sink-owned register at `kaola-workflow/{project}/.cache/final-fixes.json` — one
   entry per fix, naming the exact failed validation command, the fix commit, the touched paths, and
   the rerun receipt (same command, exit 0, bound to the recomputed post-fix candidate hash).

3. **Third attributed source.** A changed path is attributed at finalize iff it is in the allowband,
   OR in a `complete` node's declared write set, OR in a **verified** final-fix register entry.

4. **Deviation routes, never traps.** `unattributed_change` gains `route: final-fix-commit` when — and
   only when — the sink is live and pristine, and the same context-bound field is stamped on
   `would_orphan_in_progress` / `would_strand_completed_dependent` fired in finalize context.

## The scope wall: what was filed, and what the owner decided instead

> **HISTORICAL — REVERSED BY DIR-2.** Everything in this section describes decision D2 and the
> machinery built for it. That machinery has been REMOVED; the filed design (a hard wall) is what
> ships. Read this section for the argument that was tried, then read § "DIR-2 reverses D2" for why
> it was withdrawn. Nothing below is a current instruction.

**This is the honest record. The widened form below is NOT the original design.**

The filed issue drew the wall at validation apparatus. Under `## Considered and rejected` it
explicitly refused production-behavior paths through this lane, on the reasoning that the run's
standing certification covers the product, and a fix to the *product* invalidates that certification
while a fix to the *judge* does not. A production-behavior entry would have refused flatly.

**The repo owner overrode that** (decision D2 of the 2026-07-28 owner decisions). Production-behavior
fixes ARE admitted through the lane. The reasoning for the override:

- The refused case is real and recurring: a finalize-time failure is often a genuine one-line product
  defect, and forcing it out of band (a follow-up issue against a run that has not shipped, or a
  whole re-plan epoch) costs more than it protects.
- A flat wall does not actually stop the fix — it stops the *recorded* fix. The observed behavior was
  an orchestrator landing the change anyway and then spending its time fighting the sweep. A wall
  that people route around is worse than a gate they can pass honestly.

**Consequence, and the reason this is a trade and not a giveaway: the re-certification receipt is now
the load-bearing safeguard that REPLACES the wall, so it has to carry the weight the wall used to.**

A production-surface entry is admitted only when it carries a **bound re-certification receipt**: a
settled PASS review attempt over the **post-fix** candidate, digest-bound, named by the register entry
itself and read out of the run's own review journal (`readReviewJournal` — the existing settlement
machinery, inside the ledger-chain tamper boundary). It is never a self-asserted field. The verifier
refuses in four discriminated directions:

| state | what it catches |
| --- | --- |
| `missing` | the entry names no re-certification at all — an absent key is never a silent pass |
| `unresolved` | the pointer names an attempt the journal does not carry, or there is no journal (fail closed) |
| `unsettled` | the attempt exists but did not settle PASS — a failed or in-flight review is not a certification |
| `stale` | a genuine settled PASS over a **different** candidate (the receipt-replay: re-pointing at the pre-fix review), or an entry whose recorded digest disagrees with the attempt it cites |

Validation-apparatus entries (tests, fixtures, build/tooling glue, allowband docs) keep the cheap
path: the bound green rerun receipt alone, no re-review. The classifier that decides which class a
path falls into is **conservative by default — an unrecognized path is PRODUCTION**, and a mixed
entry is production as a whole. That default is the whole safety argument: an unanticipated surface
fails *toward* the re-certification wall, never through the cheap path.

## DIR-2 reverses D2: the wall stays hard

**Operative rule.** A finalize-time fix that touches PRODUCTION behavior is refused
`final_fix_production_surface`, full stop. There is no receipt, no entry field and no verifier that
admits it. The lane records **validation apparatus only** — tests, fixtures, build/tooling glue,
allowband docs — behind the bound green rerun receipt it always required.

### What was built, and what was removed

D2's machinery shipped and was then deleted in full:

| Removed | Was |
| --- | --- |
| `verifyFinalFixRecertification(entry, journal, expectedCandidateDigest)` in `kaola-workflow-adaptive-node.js`, and its export | the four-state (`missing` / `unresolved` / `unsettled` / `stale`) receipt verifier that opened the wall |
| the `recertification` branch of `runFinalFixCommit`'s RUNG 4 | read the review journal and admitted a production entry on a settled PASS bound to the post-fix candidate |
| the `recertification` slot on the recorded register entry, and the `recertification:` field on the verb's success envelope | the durable residue of the admission — and the seam a later widening would grow back through |
| the `register_entry_recertification_missing` gate in `verifyFinalFixEntry` (`kaola-workflow-adaptive-schema.js`) | admitted a production entry that carried a well-formed receipt. It is now a flat refusal on the CLASS: a production-surface entry never verifies, with or without paperwork |

Everything the wall is **not** survives untouched: the `final-fix-commit` verb, the digest-bound
sink-owned register, the third-attributed-source rule, the sink-live / pristine / green-rerun ladder,
the no-cap rule, the audit-only `role`, and the conservative classifier (an unrecognized path is
PRODUCTION; a mixed entry is production as a whole). Only ONE admission path was removed.

### Why (R4, and it is the whole argument)

ADR 0013's **R4** is the meaning-vs-form discriminator: auto-remedy applies only to a *non-canonical
FORM of correct content*. A behavior change arriving after every reviewer is discharged is not bad
form — it is a **deviation that is itself evidence**: evidence that the certification standing over
this candidate no longer describes it. R4 always beats R3, so such a deviation is *reported*, never
repaired.

`final_fix_production_surface` is an L2 content verdict protecting exactly that standing
certification. Admitting the change behind a re-certification receipt would **launder the signal**:
the finalize register would become a place where a fact about the run ("this shipped uncertified
work") is converted into a receipt saying the opposite. Building the receipt adds precisely the
re-certification machinery R4 exists to prevent, and softens the one wall this ADR names as
load-bearing.

### Why the wall is not the trap the issue complained about

D2's strongest argument was that a flat wall dead-ends: the fix is real, the run has not shipped, and
every other exit is closed by construction (§ Context). That complaint is answered **by the route,
not by widening the scope.** The refusal is typed and carries an exit —
`SINK_FINDING_ROUTE_BY_KIND.final_fix_production_surface` → `replan shape-refutation` (legacy bare
token `shape_refutation`). If no authority in the frozen plan can certify a post-discharge behavior
change, then the SHAPE is what is refuted, and the re-plan epoch that puts a certifying authority
over the work is the recorded way out. A route contract removes dead ends by construction; a wider
wall removes the *signal*.

D2's second argument — "a wall people route around is worse than a gate they can pass honestly" —
cuts the other way once the exit exists: the honest pass is the re-plan, and a register entry that
stands in for a certification is the route-around, not the cure.

### The near-miss this leaves open (deliberately unresolved here)

When the finalize sweep's unattributed set is entirely production, it still advertises
`route: final-fix-commit`. Two readings are defensible: it is a legitimate two-hop route
(`final-fix-commit` → `final_fix_production_surface` → `shape-refutation`, each hop typed), or it
violates this project's own D3/D6 doctrine that *a route is a promise the verb will accept the work*.
This reversal does not decide it, and no test pins it. It is an owner call, not an implementer's.

## The other three walls (unchanged from the filed design)

- `final_fix_sink_not_live` — the terminal `finalize` row is not `in_progress`; this run is not in
  finalization, so there is no sink to own the register.
- `final_fix_after_sink_started` — the derived sink progress is not `pristine`. **This is the lane's
  hard close.** The predicate is three-valued and fail-closed: `started` and `unknown` both refuse,
  because a false `pristine` after a push would rewrite a shipped run. After the push, recovery is a
  follow-up issue, never a history rewrite.
- `final_fix_unverified` — no green rerun receipt binding the *exact* failed command to the post-fix
  candidate. A receipt for some other command is the classic rubber stamp: it proves something
  passed, never that the thing that failed passes.

Every refusal is **zero-write**: no register created or modified, the frozen plan byte-identical, git
HEAD unmoved, the worktree unchanged. The ladder is precedence-ordered (`sink_not_live` >
`after_sink_started` > `unverified` > `production_surface`) so an operator is told the outermost fault
first — a receipt complaint about a lane that is not open at all would send them to fix the wrong
thing.

## The register is not a laundering primitive

The sweep credits the register, so an unverified register would be an attribution laundering
primitive: append a path, and an unreviewed file ships attributed. The sweep therefore **recomputes**
the register digest and re-proves every entry's own gates, and a register that does not verify refuses
its own typed reason, `final_fix_register_unverified`, zero-write — and deliberately does **not**
report the smuggled path under `unattributed_change`, whose documented cure is to delete the file. That
would be a lie about the real fault. This mirrors the argument `epoch_lineage_unverified` already makes
for the sealed parent epochs.

## Consequences

- **Accepted (post-DIR-2):** the standing certification over the product is NOT re-establishable
  mid-finalization. A production-behavior fix found at finalize costs a re-plan epoch, and that cost
  is the point: the deviation is the evidence, and the epoch is what puts a certifying authority back
  over the work. The bullet this replaces argued the opposite and rested entirely on the
  re-certification receipt being unforgeable; the receipt is gone, so the wall is again the safeguard.
- **The argument to re-read before anyone proposes this widening again** is § "DIR-2 reverses D2".
  The refused case is real and recurring — that was never in dispute. What settles it is that the
  refusal has a typed exit, so the lane does not dead-end, and that laundering the deviation into a
  receipt destroys the one signal saying the shipped candidate is uncertified.
- **Accepted:** `package.json` classifies as validation apparatus (build/tooling glue), so a
  dependency edit made during finalization takes the cheap path. Narrowing that is a follow-up if it
  ever bites.
- **No cap** on register entries per run. Each entry carries its own green rerun receipt, and that
  receipt is the natural bound; a count cap would only push a legitimate second fix out of band.
- **Custody stays prose.** A dispatched test fix goes to `tdd-guide`; the register RECORDS the
  producing role in `entry.role` (audit-only) and does not adjudicate it. A mechanical custody check
  here would re-regulate the free route the decision exists to open.
- The pristine boundary is derived, not marked: `deriveSinkProgressFromState` is now the single
  derivation shared by the adaptive node's probe and the plan validator's route, so the two
  authorities cannot disagree about whether a run has already shipped.
