# D-803-01. One live lane group at a time is retained as a deliberate simplicity choice, not an unrepaired blocker

Date: 2026-07-27
Status: Accepted — #803 declined; the single-descriptor invariant stands
Issue: #803 (declined)
Related: #802 / `D-802-01` (the principle #803 invoked: a removable blocker is a repair obligation,
         not a serializer — applied here and found to yield a different verdict once cost is weighed);
         #804 / `D-804-01` (the sibling filed with #803, accepted and shipped);
         #622 (the read-alongside-live-write relaxation that makes a second write frontier reachable);
         #615 / `D-615-01` (the baseline re-anchor rejection #803 correctly cites)

## Context

`running-set.json` carries exactly one `lane_group` descriptor. When a write lane group is live and a
second disjoint write frontier becomes ready, the scheduler excludes it wholesale —
`speculativeWriteExcluded: { reason: 'lane_group_live' }` at `kaola-workflow-adaptive-node.js:11236`,
`holdDrain('lane_group_live')` at `:11417`. Forming a second group would overwrite the live descriptor
and silently drop still-open members from tracking, so their later closes fall to the serial path,
their committed leg work never merges, and their leg worktrees leak.

#803 proposed lifting that limit: carry N descriptors (`lane_groups`), gate formation on validator-proven
disjointness across all live members, and re-base the group barrier on first-parent geometry.

Its mechanism claims were verified against the code and **all hold**:

- The descriptor is singular — `adaptive-node.js:11784`, and every reader dereferences `running.lane_group`.
- The overwrite hazard is real and pinned by regression at `test-adaptive-node.js:14885–14975`.
- The group delta is `diff(base → M)` — `kaola-workflow-plan-validator.js:6229`, `baseRev` being the
  shared leg-base ref.
- `M^1` is the pre-merge mainline tip — `adaptive-node.js:10813` merges with `--no-ff` on the feature
  branch at root, so the first parent is the branch that was checked out.
- Two groups overlapping in time genuinely break the geometry: B's leg-base predates A's merge,
  `base ∈ ancestors(M)` still passes, and `diff(B.base → M_B)` sweeps in A's merged files → a spurious
  `write_set_overflow` on a clean run.
- Re-anchoring the baseline was already rejected, and #803 cites it accurately (`D-615-01`, lines 31–33).

This decision does not dispute any of that. It declines the change on cost against unmeasured benefit.

## Decision

**Retain the one-live-lane-group invariant. `lane_group_live` stays as a structural limit, adopted
deliberately rather than tolerated as an unrepaired blocker.**

Three findings drive it.

**1. The benefit is unmeasured, and the repo's own regression asserts it is zero.**
`test-adaptive-node.js:14887` states of this exact exclusion: *"W2 simply waits… proving the fix costs
no real parallelism — only the racy overwrite is removed."* #803 asserts the opposite without measuring
the wait. The two exclusion sites do not carry equal weight:

- At the **speculative site** (`:11236`) the excluded candidate is behind a live gate. Its true
  dependency is the gate, not the group, so lifting the exclusion buys only
  `max(0, drain_time − gate_time)`. A reviewer gate typically outlasts a leg drain, making the win
  plausibly zero.
- At the **drain-hold site** (`:11417`) the candidate's dependencies are already satisfied, so the hold
  is pure loss. This is the only site with an unambiguous win, and its magnitude is unknown.

**2. There is no incremental path — the list and the geometry are structurally coupled.**
Carrying N descriptors without first-parent geometry is simply broken: group A and group B both anchor
at mainline tip `M0` (nothing commits in between), A merges to `M_A`, then B's barrier computes
`diff(M0 → M_B)` and sees A's files. Any two groups overlapping in time hit this. So D1 cannot ship
ahead of D3 to bank a partial win; it is one package or nothing.

**3. The dominant cost defends a hazard the change itself introduces.**
Today nothing commits to mainline inside a group window — merges are mutually excluded, and #802's seam
checkpoint cannot fire with a group live (`if (liveGroupId) return holdDrain(…)` at `:11417` returns
before the `parentCarriesProductionDirt` repair at `:11418`). The anti-float guarantee that `base → M`
provides is therefore free. #803 introduces sibling merges mid-window and then builds a new durable
subsystem — a transactional journal of scheduler-advance SHAs, a typed refuse on unregistered mainline
commits, and a crash-reconcile for the merge-vs-journal window — to detect the float it just created.
Plus a fourth toggle (`KAOLA_MULTI_LANE_GROUP`) in a subsystem already carrying `KAOLA_PARALLEL_WRITES`,
`KAOLA_SEAM_CHECKPOINT`, and `KAOLA_LANE_CONTAINMENT`, each doubling the verification matrix.

### Why `D-802-01` does not compel the opposite verdict

#803 invoked the #802 principle — *a precondition the orchestrator itself created is a repair obligation,
not a serializer* — and named `lane_group_live` as the next removable blocker. The principle applies, but
it is not self-executing. `D-802-01` accepted its repair because it was **cheaper than the serial
wall-clock it replaced** and mechanically checkable end-to-end. Here the wall-clock being replaced is
unquantified and the repair carries new persistent state with its own crash-consistency story. A
principle that removes blockers does not license removing one at arbitrary cost; the repair must still
be worth the surface it adds.

### Why this is not a case for agent reasoning

Lifting the limit would not hand judgment back to an agent. "Did this member's committed leg work reach
HEAD" is a git fact, and an agent that reasons about it wrongly silently loses committed work and leaks a
worktree — squarely the irreversible-effect territory where mechanical anchors belong. The machinery
#803 proposes is legitimate in **kind**; it is unjustified in **amount**. Declining it keeps the
mechanical surface small rather than trading it for agent discretion.

## Consequences

- `lane_group_live` at both sites, the single `lane_group` key, and the `diff(base → M)` group barrier
  are **load-bearing and deliberate**. A future audit finding them should read this decision before
  re-filing.
- A second ready write frontier waits for the live group to drain. That is accepted latency, not a defect.
- No toggle, no journal, no schema migration, no new crash window is added.
- `D-802-01`'s principle stands unmodified; this records that applying it yields *decline* for this
  blocker on cost grounds.

## What would reopen this

One measurement: instrument both sites and record accumulated hold time on a wide plan.

- If holds are gate-bound (the candidate was waiting on its own gate regardless), the premise fails and
  this decision is final.
- If the **drain-hold site** shows material accumulated wall-clock, reopen — and prefer the cheaper
  geometry: take `M^1 → M` for the delta and satisfy the mainline-advance whitelist from group-merge
  SHAs retained in `running-set.json`, which the scheduler already writes transactionally at close and
  already crash-reconciles. That removes the separate journal and its reconcile path, roughly halving
  the D3 surface.

## Non-goals

- Not a claim that concurrency is undesirable. Faithful decomposition and co-open by default are
  unchanged; this bounds only how many *lane groups* may be live at once.
- Not a reversal of #802 or #804, both of which shipped.
